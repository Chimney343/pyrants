# Tyrants of the Underdark — Game Manual

## Overview

Tyrants of the Underdark is a competitive deck-building area-control game for 2 or more players. Each player leads a drow house vying for dominance over the Underdark. You recruit minions, deploy troops, assassinate rivals, and promote loyal servants to your inner circle. Victory comes through a combination of territorial control, trophy kills, and the allegiances you cultivate.

The game plays in turns over multiple rounds. When the market deck runs out or any player's barracks empties entirely, the game ends. The player with the highest victory point total wins.

---

## 1. Game Components

### The Board

The Underdark is a graph of interconnected **sites** and **routes**.

- **Sites** are major locations (cities, strongholds, caverns). Each site holds up to 3 troops and has a control VP value. Sites are where you fight for dominance.
- **Routes** are paths between sites. Each route holds exactly 1 troop. Routes let you project presence into adjacent sites without occupying the site itself.
- **Adjacency**: Two nodes connected by a direct edge are adjacent. Troops on a node confer **presence** to that node and all adjacent nodes. Presence is what lets you deploy more troops and assassinate enemies in a region.
- **White troops** are neutral guards that start on some sites. They do not belong to any player and are never replenished. Killing a white troop awards a white trophy (1 VP at end of game).

### The Cards

Each card represents a follower, minion, or asset. Cards have these attributes:

| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Name** | The card's identity. |
| **Cost** | Influence required to recruit the card from the market. |
| **Aspect** | One of five factions: *Ambition, Conquest, Guile, Malice, Obedience*. Some card effects require you to reveal or hold cards of a matching aspect. |
| **Secondary Aspects** | Creature types (drow, dragon, undead, elemental, etc.). Used by certain card interactions. |
| **Deck VP** | Victory points the card is worth if it's in your deck (hand, deck, discard) at game end. |
| **Inner Circle VP** | Victory points the card is worth if it's been promoted to your inner circle at game end. |
| **Rules Text** | What the card does when you play it. |

Your starting deck contains 7 Nobles and 3 Soldiers — basic cards that generate power and influence.

### The Market

Six cards are always face-up in the market row. You spend influence to recruit them into your discard pile, where they will eventually cycle into your hand. When a card is recruited, its slot is refilled from the market deck.

There are also three **special recruit stacks** (not shown in the row, always available):

| Stack | Cost | Size | Requires |
|-------|------|------|----------|
| House Guard | 3 | 15 | Always |
| Priestess of Lolth | 3 | 15 | Always |
| Insane Outcast | 5 | 30 | Aberrations expansion enabled |

### Resources

You manage two transient resources that refill each turn:

- **Power**: Spent to deploy troops (1 power) and assassinate (3 power). Generated by card effects.
- **Influence**: Spent to recruit cards from the market. Generated by card effects.

### Player Zones

| Zone | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **Deck** | Your draw pile. Shuffled at game start and whenever it runs out. |
| **Hand** | Cards available to play this turn. Draw up to 5 at end of cleanup. |
| **Discard Pile** | Cards you've used or recruited. Shuffled into your deck when you need to draw. |
| **Played Cards** | Cards you've played this turn. They stay face-up until cleanup. |
| **Inner Circle** | Cards you've promoted. They score inner_circle_vp at game end and are removed from your deck cycle. |
| **Trophy Hall** | Player IDs of troops you've assassinated. Each trophy is worth 1 VP at game end. |
| **Barracks** | Your troop supply. Starts at 40. Deploying costs 1 barracks. When barracks reaches 0, the game ends at the end of the current round. |
| **Spies** | Spy tokens. Start at 5. Place spies on sites to gain intel. Spies count as presence for that player. |

## 2. Turn Structure

A game begins with a mandatory setup phase, followed by the main turn loop for each player:

```
SETUP (each player)  →  DRAW  →  MAIN PHASE  →  END OF TURN  →  CLEANUP  →  next player's MAIN PHASE
```

### 2.0 Setup Phase

Before any cards are dealt, each player (in turn order) must place **exactly one troop for free** on any **site** node that has an empty troop slot. Routes are not valid targets for initial placement.

- The placement costs 0 power.
- The troop is real: it consumes 1 from the player's barracks.
- Once placed, the player is marked as having completed setup.

After every player has placed their free troop, the game draws 5 cards into each player's hand and the first player enters their main phase. Subsequent players follow the normal draw-up pattern at the start of their first main phase (drawing 5 cards during the previous player's cleanup).

### 2.1 Main Phase

During the main phase you may perform any of these actions in any order:

#### Play a Card
Play one card from your hand into your played area. Its effect resolves immediately.

- Some cards have a **paid ability** (optional, costs power/influence/discards). After playing, you must either pay to activate the ability or decline it.
- Some cards have a **focus requirement**: you need another card of a matching aspect in your hand or played area (besides the card itself) for the effect to work.
- Some cards offer a **modal choice**: pick exactly one option from a list.
- Some cards let you **repeat** a choice multiple times.

#### Deploy (cost: 1 power)
Place one troop from your barracks onto an empty slot on the board.

- If your barracks is at 0, deploying instead grants 1 VP.
- Deployment requires **presence** at the target node (your troop or spy on the node, or your troop on an adjacent node) and an empty troop slot.
- The initial free troop placement during the setup phase (2.0) does not require presence and does not cost power.

#### Assassinate (cost: 3 power)
Remove one enemy troop from a node where you have presence. The removed troop goes to your trophy hall (1 VP at game end). White troops also go to trophies.

#### Recruit (cost: card's influence cost)
Buy one card from the market row (or a special stack) and place it into your discard pile.

#### Promote a Card (when prompted)
Some cards give optional promotion chances. If you promote, the card moves from your played area to your inner circle — it's out of your deck cycle but scores higher at game end. You can also decline the promotion.

#### End Main Phase
Move to the end-of-turn phase. You can do this at any time, regardless of whether you've used all available resources.

**Resource reset**: Power and influence reset to 0 at the start of each main phase. Unused resources from the previous player's main phase do not carry over.

### 2.2 End of Turn

- **Mandatory end-of-turn promotions** resolve automatically.
- **Optional/deferred promotions** are presented for you to resolve (promote or skip).
- **End-of-turn card effects** trigger: mass discards affecting opponents, scaled VP awards.
- Site scoring: for each site where you have **total control** (all troop slots filled with your troops and no enemy spies), you gain the site's per-turn VP.

### 2.3 Cleanup

1. Discard all cards from your hand and played area.
2. If your deck is empty, shuffle your discard pile to form a new deck.
3. Draw 5 cards.
4. The turn passes to the next player. If all players have acted, the round number increments.

## 3. Key Concepts

### Presence

You have presence at a node if any of these are true:
- You have at least one troop on that node.
- You have a spy on that node.
- You have at least one troop on an adjacent node (across a single route).

Presence is required to deploy troops and to assassinate.

### Control

A site's control marker belongs to the player with the most troops there. If tied, control is contested (no marker). In case of ties involving white, white never claims control unless it has a strict majority (which can only happen if marked as special — generally, white is neutral).

At the end of the game, the player holding control of a site scores its **control VP**.

### Total Control

A player has total control of a site when **all** troop slots are filled with that player's troops AND there are **no enemy spies** on the site. Total control awards per-turn VP during the end-of-turn phase.

### Spies

Spies do not occupy troop slots. They grant presence to their owner on the site. Spies can be returned to the owner's supply (for free if it's your own spy, or for 3 power if it's an enemy spy at a node where you have presence). Returned spies go back to the owner's available pool.

### Promotion

Promotion moves a card from your played area to your inner circle. The card is no longer in your deck cycle — it won't be discarded, shuffled, or drawn again. But it scores its (typically higher) inner circle VP at game end. Promotion is always optional unless the card states otherwise.

### Scaled VP

Certain cards award VP based on a metric: number of trophies, controlled sites, inner circle cards, etc. The card divides the count by a threshold and awards VP for each whole multiple. For example, "gain 1 VP for every 3 white trophies" — if you have 9 white trophies, you gain 3 VP.

## 4. Game End and Scoring

### End Conditions

The game ends at the end of the current round when either:
- The **market deck** is empty (no cards remain to refill the row).
- Any player's **barracks** reaches 0 (all troops deployed).

### Final Scoring

| Source | VP Awarded |
|--------|------------|
| Accumulated score (from in-game VP gains) | As tracked |
| Site control | Owner of each site scores its control_vp value |
| Trophies | 1 VP per trophy in your hall |
| VP tokens | As accumulated |
| Deck VP | Sum of deck_vp on cards in your deck, hand, and discard |
| Inner circle VP | Sum of inner_circle_vp on promoted cards |

The player with the highest total wins. Ties are possible — the game reports a tie with no single winner.

## 5. Playing the Game (C Engine)

### Starting a Session

```python
from engine_c.bindings.ce_api import CEngine
from engine_c.bindings.session import CSession

engine = CEngine()
engine.initialize(
    catalog_path="data/cards",
    board_path="data/boards/tyrants_of_the_underdark.json",
    setup_path="data/decks/base_setup.json",
)
session = CSession(engine, ["player_1", "player_2"], seed=42)
```

### Checking Legal Moves

```python
for move in session.legal_moves():
    print(move.move_type, move.data)
```

### Making a Move

```python
# Find the move you want from legal_moves
move = session.legal_moves()[0]
new_state = session.submit_move(move)
```

### Checking Game State

```python
if session.is_terminal():
    print(f"Winner: {session.winner_id()}")
    print(f"Scores: {session.final_scores()}")
```

## 7. Card Aspect Reference

| Aspect | Theme | Typical Effects |
|--------|-------|-----------------|
| **Ambition** | Advancement, promotion | Promoting cards, cycling deck, VP scaling |
| **Conquest** | Military dominance | Deploying troops, controlling sites, area attacks |
| **Guile** | Espionage, manipulation | Spies, forced discards, stealing, modal choices |
| **Malice** | Destruction, cruelty | Assassination, devouring, suppression, sacrifice |
| **Obedience** | Loyalty, order | Recruiting, resource generation, defense, protection |

Many cards require a **focus** — you must have another card of the same aspect visible (in hand or played) for the effect to work. Some cards also filter by secondary aspect (e.g., only promote a drow card, only affect undead troops).

## 8. Strategy Tips

1. **Early game**: Recruit cards that generate resources (power and influence). Your starter Nobles and Soldiers are weak — replace them.

2. **Board presence**: Deploy early to claim sites. Route troops can provide adjacency to multiple sites without contesting slots directly.

3. **Market awareness**: The market row changes as cards are recruited. If a key card appears, consider buying it before your opponent does.

4. **Promotion timing**: Promoting a card removes it from your deck cycle, thinning your deck. A thinner deck means you draw your best cards more often. But you lose access to that card's effect for the rest of the game.

5. **Trophy economy**: White troops are free trophies. Assassinating enemy troops both removes their board presence AND gives you VP. But at 3 power per kill, it competes with deploying your own troops.

6. **Spy placement**: Spies give you presence without using troop slots. They're excellent for maintaining presence at contested sites.

7. **Endgame awareness**: Watch your barracks count and the market deck size. Once a kill switch triggers (empty market or barracks at 0), the game ends at the end of the current round — not immediately. If you're ahead on VP, you may want to trigger the end condition. If behind, delay it.

8. **Barracks fallback**: When your barracks hits 0, every deploy action gives you 1 VP instead of placing a troop. The game continues until the end of the round, so you can still earn VP through deploys, card plays, and assassinations even with an empty barracks.

## 9. Notation

Throughout the engine and interface:

- `"white"` denotes neutral troops (not a player).
- `deck_id` / `card_id` / `node_id` are internal string identifiers.
- `aspect` is always one of: `ambition`, `conquest`, `guile`, `malice`, `obedience`.
- Market slots 100, 101, 102 are the special recruit stacks (House Guard, Priestess of Lolth, Insane Outcast).
